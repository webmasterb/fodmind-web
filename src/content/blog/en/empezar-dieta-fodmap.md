The low FODMAP diet works, but almost everyone starts it wrong. Not from lack of will: from lack of method. These are the five most repeated mistakes.

## 1. Starting elimination without a diagnosis

If everything upsets you, cutting everything seems logical. But uncontrolled elimination can mask something else (celiac disease, for example), and the diet is not a diagnosis. Doctor first, elimination after.

## 2. Staying in elimination forever

The elimination phase is not the diet: it is the preparation to find out **which FODMAPs affect you**. Living months in strict restriction impoverishes the gut microbiota and turns every meal into an act of faith. Eliminate for 2–6 weeks and reintroduce, in order.

## 3. Trusting copied-and-pasted lists

Internet lists are copied from each other, with no serving size and no source. The result: green foods that are amber in a real serving, and bans that are not bans. Every food has a **safe serving in grams**; without that figure, the list says nothing.

## 4. Forgetting that FODMAPs stack up

You can eat one amber food. What you cannot do is eat four ambers in the same meal: the load adds up. It is the most silent mistake and the easiest to fix: distribute, don't just choose.

## 5. Not writing anything down

Without a diary, reintroduction is guesswork: symptoms take up to 72 hours and memory is unreliable. Log meals and symptoms every day; after three weeks you have a map of your gut that no test can replace.
